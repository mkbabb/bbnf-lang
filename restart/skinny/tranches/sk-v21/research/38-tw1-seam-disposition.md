# B2 — The TW1a/TW1b seam adjudication: crates/core file-level disposition

Charter: SPEC-DRAFT-P1 §13.3 self-defers the TW1a(split)/TW1b(tape) ordering ("the split may reveal
that tape must move first; pass-2 critique adjudicates"). CRIT-spec-architecture B1 found TW1 was
"four waves in one" — a 247K-LOC split + AoS tape hardening + ~1,080-hit eager-builder retirement +
~960-hit StructLayout rename. The pass-1 manifest (§11) already unbundled it into **TW1a → TW1b →
TW1c**. This document (a) buckets every crates/core module by disposition, (b) quantifies each
bucket, (c) adjudicates whether split-first survives contact or tape-first moots half the split.

Method: `wc -l` over the full module tree; `rg -c` for the eager-builder (`StructBuilder`,
`push_leaf`, `TapeStructBuilder`), the rename (`StructLayout`), and the presize/dead-substrate
families, each split generated-vs-handwritten. All figures are file:count from the live tree at
HEAD `05302109d`.

## 1. The accounting that reframes everything

crates/core is **216,818 LOC of src** (247,428 with tests/benches — the CRIT figure). The composition:

| Layer | LOC | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Generated grammar parsers (`grammar/generated/`, 9 files) | 169,956 | 78.4% |
| Generated runtime accessors (`runtime/<grammar>/`, 9 dirs) | 6,931 | 3.2% |
| **Total generated (regen-owned)** | **176,887** | **81.6%** |
| **Hand-written machinery** | **39,931** | **18.4%** |

**Four out of every five lines of `core` are xtask output** (`runtime/json/value.rs:1`
`// @generated by xtask regen-json`; `grammar/generated/css_l4.rs:1` `//! AUTO-GENERATED`). This is
the load-bearing fact the whole seam adjudication turns on, and it is invisible if you read TW1 as a
247K-LOC hand-edit.

The two "big" TW1 sub-workloads dissolve under the same lens:

| Family | total hits | in generated | hand-written | generated share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `StructBuilder` (the eager-builder family) | 3,409 | 3,243 | 166 | **95.1%** |
| `StructLayout` (the "960-hit rename") | 816 (core) | 722 | 94 | **88.5%** |
| `push_leaf` (tape write API) | 978 | ~800 | ~178 | ~82% |
| `TapeStructBuilder` (the dual-encoding trap) | 7 | 0 | 7 | 0% |
| `Sampled`/`Exact`/`OneShotSimd`/`GrowOnly` (presize DELETE) | **0** | 0 | 0 | — |

The 1,080-hit eager-builder retirement and the 960-hit rename are **not hand-edit blast radii** —
they are ~40-60 emitter-template + runtime-type edits followed by a `regen` that rewrites the
generated 95%/88% for free. And the §2/§11 presize DELETE (`Sampled`/`Exact`/`OneShotSimd`) that
TW1c is said to carry **has zero occurrences in `core`** — it lives in `crates/simd-scan`/`crates/ir`
and never touches the split.

## 3. Adjudication: does splitting first shrink the blast radius, or does tape-first moot the split?

### 3a. Tape-first is NOT forced — the seam is cuttable with the tape still dual-encoded

The §13.3 fear is that the split "reveals tape must move first" — i.e. a runtime↔codegen dependency
cycle that only the tape hardening can break. It does not exist. The emitter emits **source text**
(the identifier `StructBuilder`/`push_leaf` as a string); it does not link `runtime::tape`. Only the
**generated parsers** link the tape. So the dependency shape is acyclic and one-directional:
`codegen` (emits text) → `grammar/generated` (links) → `runtime::tape`. The dual-encoding trap
`TapeStructBuilder` is a 7-hit *runtime* type; deleting it is a within-`runtime`-crate edit that needs
no codegen change beyond the template that spells it. **The runtime/codegen boundary can be cut while
the tape is still in its bad dual-encoding form.** Tape-first buys no un-blocking.

### 3b. Split-first shrinks the TW1b blast radius; the "mooting" concern is real but bounded

Splitting first gives TW1b two small, clean crates to edit — `runtime` (tape 858 + builders/arenas
2,749) and the `shapes/*/struct_direct.rs` templates in `codegen` — instead of surgery inside a fused
247K crate where a parity regression cannot be attributed to a boundary. That is the §8/report-20
thesis (land substrate into clean crates, don't re-fuse), and it holds for TW1b/TW1c exactly as it
holds for TW3/TW4.

The counter — "tape-first means you regen once and split stable content, versus split-then-regen-
twice" — is **true but cheap**, and only for the generated 81.6%. TW1a relocates 176,887 LOC of
generated code; TW1b regens it (new builder shape); TW1c regens it again (renamed Layout). That is
"place, then rewrite twice." But the rewrite is a mechanical `xtask regen` + `git`-clean check, not
labor. The concern would bite only if TW1a spent effort **hand-partitioning the generated tree by
concern** — which it must not: the generated tree is already partitioned by grammar (9 parsers × 9
accessor dirs) and is a *payload*, not a decomposition target. The decomposition seams apply to the
**39,931 hand-written LOC only**; that hand-written machinery is *not* churned by TW1b/TW1c and *is*
the split's actual value.

### 3c. Verdict

**Split-first survives contact. Keep the manifest order TW1a → TW1b → TW1c.** The unbundling into
three sub-waves is correct, and (2)+(3) are correctly fused into TW1b: retiring the eager builders IS
the AoS hardening — Lock 1's "exactly one encoding" is *defined* as deleting the dual-encoding
`TapeStructBuilder` and regenerating single-writer builders (§4). They cannot be separated; they are
one substrate landing. TW1c is correctly separated as the mechanical tail — a pure rename whose 88%
is regen and whose carried presize DELETE does not even touch `core`.

Two binding amendments make the ordering safe:

- **A1 — Scope-bound TW1a to the hand-written seam.** TW1a partitions the 39,931 hand-written LOC
  across the six seams and relocates the 176,887-LOC generated tree as a **bulk `git mv` payload**
  (per grammar), NOT a hand-curated split. Its close is proven by regen producing **byte-identical**
  output post-move (pure relocation, zero behavior).
- **A2 — Declare TW1b/TW1c regen-owned, not hand-edited.** Their headline counts (≈1,080 / ≈960) are
  95%/88% generated; the true hand-edit surface is ~40-60 template/type sites in `runtime`+`codegen`,
  crate-local after TW1a. The wave manifest must state the counts as regen artifacts so no reviewer
  reads them as 2,000-site hand blast radii (the CRIT B1 misread) and re-bundles them.

## 4. Consumer-invariant gate per sub-wave

Per §1.1 and CRIT-spec-architecture R1, TW1a is a **structural** wave (no emitted-decision surface of
its own) and closes on the *structural-lock* class; TW1b is a **substrate** wave and closes on a
*consumer-invariant runtime* gate; TW1c is structural again.

- **TW1a (split) — structural-lock class.** Widened Lock-14 keyed on the **new crate directories**
  (frontend/grammar · codegen · runtime · kernels · passes · host), not on the phantom crate paths
  Lock-14 names today (report 20 §1a); `+` build green both workspaces; `+` **9-grammar regen
  byte-IDENTICAL** (the relocation-only proof); `+` differential oracle green across all 9 grammars.
  No performance clause — a pure move must not move a number.

- **TW1b (tape + eager-builder) — consumer-invariant runtime gate.** The tape's consumer is every
  grammar's regenerated builder: gate = **JSON + Sheets oracles field-for-field** `+` Lock 1 green,
  falsified two ways (`grep`-zero on `TapeStructBuilder` AND a runtime assertion that exactly one
  owner stamps `frame_depth`, per §4/13-D1) `+` **no cold regression vs the TW0 baseline**. Note the
  one relaxation of the Y.13 "≥3% win" clause: TW1b *restores* the AU AoS floor (§2), so its gate is
  liveness-AND-no-regression, not liveness-AND-≥3% — the win is recovering ground SoA lost, and the
  spec should say so at the gate rather than let the ≥3% template make the gate unmeetable.

- **TW1c (rename + reclaim) — structural-lock class.** Regen **byte-clean vs the new golden** `+`
  `grep`-zero on the retired `StructLayout` name `+` `grep`-zero on the dead `dta`/`tape::visitor`
  residue and the `attach_structural_index` no-op `+` no cold regression. The presize DELETE it
  carries closes in `crates/simd-scan`/`crates/ir`, not `core`, and should be re-homed to a
  simd-scan-scoped gate rather than listed under a `core`-split tail.

---

## 5. One-paragraph recommendation

Keep **TW1a → TW1b → TW1c**; the split-first ordering is sound and tape-first is not forced (no
runtime↔codegen cycle — codegen emits text, not links). The adjudication's real yield is dispelling
the 247K/1080/960 framing: `core` is 81.6% xtask output, the eager-builder retirement and the rename
are 95%/88% regen-owned, so TW1b/TW1c's true hand-edit surface is ~40-60 template/type sites and
becomes *smaller* — not moot — once TW1a gives them clean `runtime` and `codegen` crates. Bind the two
amendments (A1: TW1a splits only the 39.9K hand-written seam, relocates the generated tree as a bulk
regen-owned payload proven byte-identical; A2: declare the 1,080/960 counts regen artifacts) and the
seam is plan-ready.
