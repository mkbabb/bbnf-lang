## §1 — Findings (concrete; file:line on bbnf claims, citation on external claims)

1. Lock 1's answer is yes: the retained tape and the retained structural projection are one substrate. Lock 1 says retained structural offsets are tape, direct-only `SinkOnly` may retain no queryable document identity, and parallel substrates/sidecars are dead (`restart/locks/LOCKS.md:52`). The live runtime tape stores the source slice, one `Vec<u32>` offset stream, sparse flag cursors/values, payload arena, and tape id (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/tape/mod.rs:94`-`100`); `ValueRef` is only tape plus cursor (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/tape/mod.rs:175`-`180`, `191`-`222`). JSON node kind is rediscovered from `source[offsets[cursor]]`, not from a retained class lane (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/grammars/json/value.rs:28`-`47`). The generated parser state has input, bytes, cursor, and `TapeBuilder` only (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/grammars/json/parser.rs:7`-`12`), and the generated recognizer hook is a no-op attachment, not a retained index (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/grammars/json/generated.rs:10`-`17`).

2. Track 1 and Track 2 retained parse agree on the same tape substrate. Track 1 constructs `ParserState`, calls generated parse, and seals `JsonRoot` from the builder (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/grammars/json/parser.rs:46`-`52`). Track 2 is an independent hand-coded parser, but it also owns `TapeBuilder`, plans capacity through the same JSON scan helper, and seals `JsonRoot` from that tape (`skinny/crates/bbnf-bench/src/track2/json.rs:14`-`33`, `36`-`44`). Its compatibility test asserts Track 2 offsets equal Track 1 offsets without calling Track 1 (`skinny/crates/bbnf-bench/src/track2/json.rs:357`-`363`). The gate reports the same fact: Track 1 is `runtime::generated_json::parse`, Track 2 is independent over `runtime::tape`, and Track 2 uses `runtime::tape::TapeBuilder` (`skinny/RESULTS.md:144`-`145`; emitted by `skinny/crates/bbnf-bench/src/bin/gate.rs:337`-`343`).

3. Lazy materialization counters support the offset-tape design, but they do not admit rows. All 17 W0 materialization notes report zero payload bytes (`skinny/RESULTS.md:96`-`142`), and P1-C records matching Track 1/Track 2 summaries with zero payload bytes for all 17 corpora (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1c-samply-mode-3.md:114`-`138`). Logical tape ratios peak at `y_string_unicode` 0.50x input, `marine_ik` 0.48x, `mesh` 0.44x, and `canada` 0.40x; allocated tape ratios peak at `y_string_unicode` 0.75x, `mesh` 0.72x, and `marine_ik` 0.70x (`skinny/RESULTS.md:102`, `114`, `122`, `142`). Sparse escape flag pressure is concentrated in unicode/string rows: `unicode_mixed` 9795, `unicode_escapes` 9385, `y_string_unicode` 9000, and `gsoc-2018` 8545 sparse flag bytes (`skinny/RESULTS.md:120`, `131`, `134`, `142`; summarized at `restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1e-hot-leaf-attribution.md:230`-`237`). These are substrate-shape facts, not behavior admissions (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/hardening/HARDENING-S-P1-CONVERGED.md:46`-`48`).

4. Direct Track 1/Track 2 are output-plane consumers, not alternate retained substrates. The result rows label direct output as `digest` and describe "generated Track 1 SinkOnly vs independent hand Track 2 SinkOnly" (`skinny/RESULTS.md:6`, `12`, `17`, `20`, `23`, `35`, `37`, `91`; gate construction at `skinny/crates/bbnf-bench/src/bin/gate.rs:199`-`209`). The bench Track 1 direct path calls `runtime::generated_json::parse_direct`, Track 2 calls the independent hand parser, and parity compares both to serde/sonic digest shape (`skinny/crates/bbnf-bench/src/direct_struct.rs:401`-`435`). The generated direct parser passes source bytes and a local cursor to a `JsonSink` (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/grammars/json/generated.rs:407`-`464`), while the hand Track 2 direct parser keeps only bytes and cursor (`skinny/crates/bbnf-bench/src/direct_struct.rs:440`-`457`). This fits Lock 1's `SinkOnly` clause instead of proving a second substrate.

5. The typed direct plane is also a product consumer, not a tape fork. W0 reports `real_typed_struct` rows as typed direct output with generated Track 1 consuming a host/API output schema and Track 2 as structural/oracle context (`skinny/RESULTS.md:7`, `10`, `18`, `21`, `24`). The generated typed module is a consumer-crate `DirectBuild` parser over `&str`, parse-that string/number primitives, and local cursor state (`skinny/crates/bbnf-bench/src/generated_real_typed.rs:1`-`14`, `31`-`40`, `1593`-`1607`). Its hot typed skip/string/number leaves are guard/product-plane evidence, not a new retained representation (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1b-samply-mode-2.md:233`-`247`).

6. W3 is not a candidate substrate repair. REDRESS 96 added a co-indexed class column and move-consumed structural positions, went correctness-green, then failed every W3 must-improve and W10b maintain row (`skinny/REDRESS.md:2797`-`2847`). REDRESS 97 replaced the full vector with an allocation-free streaming cursor and still failed every binding row (`skinny/REDRESS.md:2852`-`2906`). REDRESS 98 retires `G-W3-UNION-SUBSTRATE`: on this host, source-byte rediscovery is cheaper than routing a SIMD structural cursor through retained parsing (`skinny/REDRESS.md:2910`-`2949`). REDRESS 102 keeps parse-only W3 proof out of behavior/source/generated-output row movement and names union/event substrate, retained class column, structural/streaming cursor, and parser-owned structural projection as closed routes (`skinny/REDRESS.md:3042`-`3058`). Therefore P2-D candidate space is existing offset tape plus existing direct/typed consumers only.

7. The row-moving pressure is work per byte and consumer shape, not lack of a second structural store. P1-D reports wide-issue execution, with aggregate direct CPI 0.212 and typed CPI 0.190 (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1d-pmu-cycles.md:89`-`93`), and concludes the target pressure is work per byte and data movement rather than a retained sidecar cursor (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1d-pmu-cycles.md:224`-`240`). P1-E's accepted residual families are unicode/string escape, tiny strings/digest, number/container rows, and cross-cutting whitespace/dispatch (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1e-hot-leaf-attribution.md:277`-`284`), with W3/class-column/cursor temptations pre-blocked (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1e-hot-leaf-attribution.md:250`-`259`, `315`-`322`).

## §3 — Grammar-neutrality (each candidate: JSON-only or CSS/Sheets/BBNF-self generalisable)

- D1 `container_tail_next`: Generalisable. The primitive is "after an element/member, skip grammar whitespace and classify one of a configured separator/close byte set." CSS declaration lists, Sheets array/list forms, and BBNF comma/semicolon lists can express the byte sets in grammar metadata. It must not bake JSON punctuation into a generic crate; Lock 14 requires the sets to come from source/metadata (`restart/locks/LOCKS.md:78`).

- D2 `direct_slot_dispatch`: Generalisable through existing `DirectBuild` and `SinkOnly` lowering. The slot names are backend-lowering concepts over generated output shapes, not JSON public API. It is JSON-overfit only if implemented as a generic-crate `match Json`; that is forbidden by Lock 14 (`restart/locks/LOCKS.md:78`).

- D3 `borrowed_string_span`: Generalisable as a delimited-span primitive with escape policy supplied by grammar metadata. JSON strings, CSS strings/idents that need escape validation, Sheets quoted strings, and BBNF-self string/token literals can use the same scalar-ref shape with different delimiter/control/escape tables. It is JSON-only if the escape alphabet is hard-coded outside generated grammar runtime.

- D4 `number_span_emit_slot`: Generalisable for grammars with numeric token families, provided the numeric grammar is declarative. JSON, CSS numbers/dimensions, Sheets numeric literals, and BBNF-self numeric metadata can share a scan/materialize boundary while preserving grammar-specific validity rules. It is not a substrate change.

- D5 `tape_sparse_flag_delta_lane`: Generalisable because it changes how the tape encodes sparse facts, not which grammar facts exist. The generic `EventGrammar` surface already describes grammar structural class/fact admission without naming JSON (`skinny/crates/runtime/src/tape/event_grammar.rs:4`-`14`). Per Lock 14, the flag meanings must be generated from grammar metadata or per-grammar runtime, never hard-coded in generic runtime code (`restart/locks/LOCKS.md:78`).

## §4 — Risks (REDRESS entries any candidate must NOT re-open)

- REDRESS 50: do not reintroduce dense per-cursor aux columns or sparse side tables for retained view projection; parser-time side tables improved eager probes but regressed the governing parse plane (`skinny/REDRESS.md:717`-`740`). D1/D5 must stay inside the live cursor loop or the tape-owned sparse flag stream.

- REDRESS 51 and 53: do not rename whitespace/structural scanning as an event cursor, structural index, bitmap, aux projection, or parser-local structural cursor (`skinny/REDRESS.md:742`-`767`, `784`-`812`). D1 may factor scalar delimiter handling, but it cannot retain or stream a second structural substrate.

- REDRESS 96, 97, and 98: no class-column tape, move-consumed structural position vector, streaming cursor, class-lane-only repair, or revived W3 union-substrate wave (`skinny/REDRESS.md:2797`-`2847`, `2852`-`2906`, `2910`-`2949`). D1 and D5 are valid only because they preserve source-offset classification and existing tape ownership.

- REDRESS 102: parse-only, structural-scan-only, masking, PMU, and lazy-tape counters remain diagnostics and cannot admit product rows (`skinny/REDRESS.md:3042`-`3058`; `restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/p1f-results-delta.md:221`-`225`). D5 especially must not be sold as a direct close from ratio improvement alone.

- P1 hardening: direct residuals are the primary closure surface, typed rows are guards/product credibility, W0-clamped rows need behavior-wave provenance, and non-JSON telemetry is still absent (`restart/skinny/tranches/sk-v11/research/p1/hardening/HARDENING-S-P1-CONVERGED.md:34`-`55`). Any S-P3 packet using D1-D5 needs same-wave generated direct or typed consumer evidence, not only a microbench.
