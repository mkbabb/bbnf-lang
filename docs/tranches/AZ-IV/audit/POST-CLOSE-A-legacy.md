## Findings

### Category 1 — Legacy survivors

The largest legacy surface is the **typed-path-to-`LegacyPath` re-lowering shim duplicated across all four W3 grammars**. Every `parse_with(input, &path)` entry constructs a typed-path executor, drives the cursor-threaded dispatcher, finalises the document, then **re-lowers each typed segment back into the pre-W3 borrowed `runtime::path::PathSegment` alphabet** to call the document's pre-W3 `doc.get::<T>(LegacyPath::new(&legacy))`. Four `lower()` helper functions, four imports of `Path as LegacyPath, PathSegment as LegacySegment`, four `Vec<LegacySegment<'_>>::with_capacity(path.len())` allocations on the lazy-fast-path. The "Lower a typed-path segment into the legacy borrowed alphabet" docstring is byte-identical across `crates/core/src/runtime/{json,bbnf,css_l4,google_sheets}/parse_with.rs`. The W2 typed `TypedPath<G,T>` invariant (Hard Gate 7) lifts the path; the W3 lazy entry then drops it back to the pre-W2 alphabet because `JsonDocument::get` / equivalent was never re-typed to consume `TypedPath`.

### Category 2 — Shims surviving close

The **`__EAGER_EMPTY_PATH` LazyLock with hardcoded `markers::Json` for every grammar** is the most architecturally significant shim. Every emitted struct-direct parse body (9 grammars: json, bnf, csv, ebnf, math, google_sheets, bbnf, css_pretty, css_l4) instantiates:

```rust
static __EAGER_EMPTY_PATH: ::std::sync::LazyLock<
    crate::path::ir::TypedPath<crate::path::markers::Json, &'static str>,
> = ...;
```

— `markers::Json` is the marker for **every** non-JSON grammar's eager-cursor empty-path scaffolding. The `crates/core/src/backend/rust/emitter/grammar.rs:414-425` emit site hardcodes the marker to `Json` regardless of the grammar being lowered. The cursor's state is unused (the closure returns `ParseFully` unconditionally); the only reason the marker matters is to satisfy the HRTB `__P: for<'__c> PathSchema<'__c>` lifetime decoupling. This is a cross-grammar leak: a literal `JSON_GRAMMAR` reference in nine grammars' generated code, surviving the W1 grammar-overfit static scan because the scan inspects `crates/core/src/runtime/**` and `crates/core/src/backend/rust/emitter/shapes/**` only — not `crates/core/src/grammar/generated/**`.

### Category 4 — Contrived complexity

The **HRTB `__P: for<'__c> PathSchema<'__c>` propagation across ~264 generated dispatcher signatures** is the most pervasive complexity surviving close. The lifetime decoupling is genuine (eager-mode parses construct the cursor function-local against an empty path; tying the cursor's schema lifetime to `'p` would propagate the function-local borrow through the document return), but the HRTB carries cross-cutting drag. The W3.6 reconciliation commit (`5cd6e5d9`) lifted it via `LazyLock<TypedPath<Json, &'static str>>` — yet **the marker is hardcoded `Json`** for every grammar (Category 2 finding 2.1). The simpler architectural fix: the cursor doesn't need a generic schema at all if the eager lane provides a no-op cursor; the `Decision::ParseFully`-only closure would carry no schema-lifetime requirement.

### Category 5 — Special cases

The **2 Sheets Flat-shape `#[ignore]` tests** (post-W3 carry F1) are the W3 close's named miss. The reason is mechanically real: the Flat-shape compound doesn't consult Object/Array `cursor.match_field`/`match_index` because Flat compounds have no field/index alphabet. The architecturally clean closure is a Flat-shape early-bail when the cursor reaches its terminal mid-body — distinct from the W3-DYNAMIC mechanism. The `#[ignore]` is justified per Hard Gate 6's owner+deadline+reason+ticket triplet (commit `715747db`); the architectural concern is that **the lazy-error-elision contract has a Flat-shape blind spot**. Sheets formula evaluation through lazy parse cannot honor errors-past-path-reach; eager parse fails, lazy succeeds.

## Recommended Path Forward

The triage routes naturally into three follow-on commits, each scoped to a coherent architectural surface. Each is ≤ 1 hr write-window and lands as one commit per `commit-discipline`.

### Commit 1 — `refactor(runtime/parse_with): widen JsonPathQuery to consume TypedPath; delete LegacyPath shim across 4 grammars`

Removes the `LegacyPath` / `LegacySegment` re-lowering shim from `crates/core/src/runtime/{json,bbnf,css_l4,google_sheets}/parse_with.rs`. Widens `JsonDocument::get<T>` (and the 3 sibling document `get` traits) to consume `TypedPath<G, T>` directly. Deletes the four `lower()` helpers, the four `Vec<LegacySegment>::with_capacity` allocations, the four `runtime::path::PathSegment as LegacySegment` imports. Net: ~80 LOC deleted, one allocation per lazy parse eliminated, the legacy borrowed alphabet retires (or restricts to internal eager use). Closes findings 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 2.3.

### Commit 2 — `refactor(emitter/grammar): per-grammar markers::G in __EAGER_EMPTY_PATH; collapse HRTB via eager/lazy cursor split`

Replaces `markers::Json` hardcode at `crates/core/src/backend/rust/emitter/grammar.rs:414-425` with the grammar-derived marker (per-grammar projection from `EmitStrategy`). Splits `PathCursor` into a zero-sized `EagerCursor` (no schema, no path) and the existing `PathCursor<P>` for lazy. Eliminates the HRTB `for<'__c> PathSchema<'__c>` from ~264 dispatcher signatures (`cursor_param.rs::cursor_where_clause` collapses; `cursor_generic_clause` becomes the per-mode marker). The dispatcher signature simplifies; the LazyLock-static-empty-path scaffolding evaporates. Closes findings 2.1, 4.1, 5.2.

### Commit 3 — `excise(strategy + scc + serialize): delete dead substrate; gate byte_skip_value emit on consumes`

Deletes `crates/core/src/backend/strategy/{ref,seq,repeat}_strategy.rs` + `mod.rs::{NodeStrategy, SeqResultStrategy}`, `crates/core/src/graph/scc.rs::{calculate_acyclic_deps_scc, calculate_non_acyclic_deps_scc}`, `crates/core/src/generate/serialize/mod.rs::generate_serialize_methods`. Migrates the `cursor.with_ascent` family + `crates/core/src/path/ascent.rs` (277 LOC) into either consumed (W3 lazy wildcard re-anchor) or deleted; the W2 `path_ascent.rs` bench either drives a real ascent or is deleted alongside. Routes `byte_skip_value` emission through a consumes-gate: only grammars whose IR actually has `cursor.match_field`/`match_index` consult sites get the helper; CSV / math / css_pretty / google_sheets stop emitting ~250 LOC × 5 of dead skip helpers. Closes findings 1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 1.8, 2.2, 2.4, 3.4, 5.3.

A fourth optional commit lands `path_seed_rewrites` consumer wiring (3.1) into production saturation OR deletes the seed entirely; absent a real saturation consumer, the rule storage is dead-on-arrival per Invariant 3. Findings 3.2 (`bbnf-path-ts` consumer wiring) and 5.1 (Flat-shape lazy honoring) route to BC tranche (playground/extension TS integration) and to a focused W3 follow-on respectively, per the AZ-IV close's named carries — both are post-cleanup tranche scope, not surgical post-close work.

The 32 zero-caller substrates from W5.4 (substrate-audit-pass.txt) split: Commit 3 above retires 6 (the strategy + scc + serialize families); the test-only callers (10 items per `W5-substrate-denominator.md` §Test/bench-only) migrate to `pub(crate)` + `cfg_attr(any(test, feature = "_internal"), ...)` re-export; the remaining ~16 `pub`-misclassified internal helpers (`phf_dispatch_fn_ident`, `cursor_generic_clause`, etc.) tighten to `pub(super)` / `pub(crate)`. Substrate-audit then passes with zero `SANCTIONED_SUBSTRATES` entries.
