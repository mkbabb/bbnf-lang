### B-R3-04 — time-based stale-lock reclamation can remove a live publisher's lock

- **Severity:** HIGH.
- **Exact file/evidence basis:** `xtask/src/architecture/evidence/transaction.rs:247-266`
  declares a lock stale solely from file modification age and removes the lock
  path without checking the recorded process, a lease token, or owner
  liveness. `transaction.rs:279-282` then lets every `LockGuard` remove that
  path unconditionally on drop. A paused or slow live owner can therefore lose
  its lock after the fixed age; a second publisher can acquire a new file at
  the same path, and the first owner's later drop can delete the second
  publisher's lock. The failpoint bank at
  `xtask/tests/architecture/evidence.rs:238-327` checks individual publication
  failures and corrupt-current rollback but has no active-old-owner,
  reclamation-token, or old-owner-drop case. The two ordinary simultaneous
  publishers reported by the author do not exercise this composition.
- **Scope:** publication/locking, concurrent durability, `CURRENT` atomicity,
  quarantine installation, and exact receipt trust.
- **Owning mechanism:** `LockGuard` ownership and stale-lock recovery in
  `evidence/transaction.rs`.
- **Disposition:** **REPAIR.** Use an operating-system lock or an
  ownership-token/lease protocol whose reclamation verifies owner death and
  whose drop removes only the lock instance it acquired. Add active-owner,
  expired-owner, replacement-owner, and crash-recovery compositions before
  repeating concurrency and failpoint evidence.
