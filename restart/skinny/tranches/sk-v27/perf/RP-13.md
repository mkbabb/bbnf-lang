## What the 1.56x is, and is not

- **It is** the ratio by which the emitted JSON parser was *slower* than parse-that's hand-written `json_parser()` on canada, the all-float corpus, with the numeric-leaf probe applied. The same record shows the emitted parser *faster* on data (0.70–0.72x) and twitter (0.83–0.85x), and tied on citm (1.00–1.03x).
- **It is not** a competitor result: no simd-json, serde_json or yyjson arm is in it. It is not a SIMD-dispatch result either, since neither arm calls `simd-scan` on numbers. It certifies nothing about the sk-v27 base, because the measured tree is not on `origin/master` and the patch is on no branch.
- **The governing cause recorded** (LOCK-4 REGISTRY gap 1): the emitted leaf feeds an unpadded `input.as_bytes()`. So the incumbent's 16-byte digit stripe cannot be ported without a padded-input `parse()` contract. That contract is LW5.PERF-1 (U10: canada attribution across lexing, float conversion, tape writes and allocation; the lazy-number tape as its own plane).

## Disposition

- **Retract** every use of "canada 1.56x" as a standing property of bbnf-lang. Allowed uses are only the scoped one: "LOCK-4 4B probe, ce51c36fc + uncommitted numeric-leaf patch, emitted/incumbent, fastest-sample, NOISY".
- **Keep** the attribution hypothesis (padded input plus SIMD digit stripe) as LW5.PERF-1's first plane, to be measured, not assumed.
- The re-measure at the sk-v27 base is below. It is the number future records cite until LW2's frozen floors replace it.
