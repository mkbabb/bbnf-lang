# Tranche B3 — AY-II.W0' Revert + Parser-Baseline Restoration

B3 reverts the AY-II.W0' source landings (FusedBuilder collapse, materializer
routing, scan-policy splice, and the dependent test + tape refactors) to
restore the pre-W0' parser baseline. The reverted commits are preserved
verbatim in B3 audit so they can re-land as B4 under the post-B2 xtask
substrate, where bisect-and-fix is straightforward (no proc-macro re-compile
cost between bisect steps). B3 is a focused two-wave revert tranche with no
successor debt tree of its own; once it closes, B2 resumes from W0.c
re-execution on a known-good runtime substrate.

## Architectural thesis

The four 2026-04-25 deep audits (`docs/audit/2026-04-25-deep-audit/AUDIT-{A,B,C,D}-*.md`)
attributed the 80+ minute cold rustc-side wall to "AY-II IR-pipeline
accumulation running inside rustc's expand phase". B2's xtask transposition
(T3) was the prescribed remedy: relocate the IR pipeline out of rustc's
expand phase into a build-time `cargo xtask regen` step, then retire the
proc-macro entirely.

B2.W0.c's diagnostic probe disproves that attribution. Re-running
`cargo xtask regen --grammar bbnf` (which invokes
`bbnf::pipeline::compile_paths_request` from a release-profile xtask binary,
with no proc-macro expansion in flight) hangs > 5 min. The agent's narrowing
proves the wall lives in `BbnfBootstrap::parse` itself — the runtime parser
on `bbnf.bbnf`-shaped corpora — not in the IR pipeline. The same regression
reproduces against the smaller `json` grammar (537 bytes), which hung
> 39 s in W0.c without completing.

The actual root cause is a runtime parser regression introduced when AY-II's
W0' source landings cherry-picked onto master without a corresponding
`generated.rs` regen. The W0' commits collapsed `TapeBuilder` + `ValueBuilder`
into `FusedBuilder` (W0'.a), routed materializer lookups through raw rule
names (W0'.b), and spliced `STRUCTURAL_SCAN_POLICY` into the codegen path
(W0'.c). Each of those landings affects every compound write or scan
boundary; together they compound on `bbnf.bbnf`-shaped grammars (deep
recursion, dense alternation) into a runtime regression invisible to the
pre-W0' `compile_pipeline` bench's 911 µs `compile_bbnf` measurement.

B2 alone cannot close on this substrate. T3 relocates the wall from
rustc-expand-time to xtask-runtime, but the wall persists. Every B2 hard
gate that measures regen wall-clock observes the same regression at xtask
runtime that pre-B2 observed at rustc-expand time.

B3's response: **revert the parity-critical runtime architecture changes,
restore the known-good substrate, and let B2 land on a runtime where its
hard gates can close cleanly**. The reverted W0' work re-lands as B4 on the
post-B2 xtask substrate, where:

1. The 80+ min cold rustc-expand wall is gone (B2 retired it).
2. Bisect cycles cost seconds rather than hours (`cargo xtask regen` runs
   the IR pipeline once per cycle; the consumer crates `include!` the
   on-disk product without re-running expansion).
3. The runtime parser regression is bisectable against the BBNF parse test
   directly, with bench evidence at every step.

This is not a workaround. The architectural posture is: **a runtime parser
regression on parity-critical types must be diagnosed against a substrate
where bisect cycles are tractable**. The pre-B2 substrate is not such a
substrate; the post-B2 substrate is. B3 re-orders the work to match.

## Operational posture

1. **B3 dispatches AFTER B2.W0.c partial-close.** Master HEAD at B3 open
   is `b8cacedd` (the B2.W0.c status-snapshot commit). The xtask substrate
   landed at `dec67806`; the per-grammar boundary spec at `3c68e8c4`; the
   W0.c partial-close (xtask body + bbnf-bootstrap migration) at
   `21881591`. All three commits stay on master through B3.
2. **B3 executes in two waves.** W0 (revert sequence + build verification),
   W1 (parity verification + bench + close). Master green at both wave
   boundaries. No delete-then-swap window.
3. **The revert is `git revert`-mediated, not `git reset`.** B3 produces
   forward commits on master that undo W0' content. History stays linear;
   no force-push, no destructive operation. The `git revert` form preserves
   the original commits in history so B4 can cherry-pick them onto the
   post-B2 substrate.
4. **Single-cargo-per-target discipline.** B3 dispatches one revert agent
   in W0 (sequential `git revert` chain with conflict resolution) and one
   verification agent in W1. No parallel cargo invocations.
5. **No mid-tranche scope absorption.** If the W0' revert does not restore
   parser baseline, B3.W0's escape clause expands the revert scope to
   include AY-II.W0-fix (`f8ac2cd7`, `c9142405`) and, if necessary, the
   AY-II.W0 base commits. Each expansion is documented in PROGRESS.md.
   Beyond W0 base, the revert exhausts the candidate set: if performance
   still doesn't restore, B3 closes on what landed and the residual scope
   opens B5 (B4 is reserved for W0' re-land).
6. **No new tranche-letter contention with B4.** B4 is the named successor
   for W0' re-land. It opens after B2 closes (B2 → B3 → B2 resume → B4 is
   the sequencing). B3 carries no B4 work.
7. **AY-II planning continuity.** AY-II.W0' close ceremony as authored
   under AUDIT-B no longer applies in its original form — the W0' source
   landings being reverted ARE the AY-II.W0' work. The compressed-honest
   close ceremony shifts to land as B4's close, not AY-II.W0'. AY-II.W1-W5
   sequencing remains unchanged; those waves operate on whatever substrate
   B4 produces.

## Defensible floor

B3's defensible floor:

1. The 14 W0'-scope `git revert` commits land on master with explicit
   per-commit messages citing the original SHA.
2. `cargo nextest run --workspace --profile ax-iter` exits 0 at B3 close.
3. `BbnfBootstrap::parse` on `bbnf.bbnf` succeeds in < 5 s wall (release
   profile).
4. `cargo xtask regen --grammar bbnf` (the B2.W0.c invocation that hung)
   completes in < 5 min cold under the post-B3 substrate.
5. `docs/tranches/B3/audit/W0p-revert-record.md` exists and lists every
   reverted commit + diff path.

Anything less leaves the parser regression in place, and B2 cannot resume
W0.c. If the floor cannot close, B3 escalates to user direction (the
candidate set for the regression — W0' alone, W0' + W0-fix, W0' + W0-fix +
W0 base — is exhausted by step 1's scope expansion; further escalation
beyond W0 base is a hard environmental blocker, not scope-reveal).

## Escape clause

**Escape 1 — W0' revert insufficient.** If post-W0-revert,
`BbnfBootstrap::parse` still hangs > 5 s on `bbnf.bbnf`, the parser
regression's root cause sits earlier than W0'. B3.W0 expands scope to
include AY-II.W0-fix (`f8ac2cd7`, `c9142405`) and re-runs the verification.
Each scope expansion lands as additional revert commits + a PROGRESS.md
entry naming the expansion + new wall measurement.

**Escape 2 — W0-fix revert insufficient.** If post-W0-fix-revert the
parser still hangs, scope expands to AY-II.W0 base (the W0.a/b/c/d/e
commits introducing `begin_compound` + `ValueBuilder` + `STRUCTURAL_SCAN_POLICY`).
This is the floor of the candidate set. PROGRESS.md records the
expansion; a fresh test wall measurement lands.

**Escape 3 — full W0 base revert insufficient.** The regression sits
outside the AY-II.W0/W0' window — likely in B1, in dependency
upgrades, or in nightly drift. B3 closes on what landed; the residual
scope opens **B5** (B4 is reserved for W0' re-land). B5's plan is
authored under the orchestrator's direction at that point; B3 does not
pre-author it.

**No mid-tranche thesis pivot beyond the revert.** B3's thesis is the
revert. If execution surfaces work that contradicts the revert (e.g., a
B2.W0.a/b/c commit conflicts irrecoverably with the revert chain), B3
closes on what landed and the conflict opens B5 with a fresh thesis.

2. `cargo nextest run --workspace --profile ax-iter` exits 0.
3. `BbnfBootstrap::parse` on `bbnf.bbnf` returns in < 5 s wall.
4. `cargo xtask regen --grammar bbnf` completes in < 5 min cold.

6. `docs/tranches/REMAINING-TRAJECTORY.md` and `docs/tranches/AY-II/PATH-FORWARD.md`
   reflect B3 close + B4 forward pointer.

Then the orchestrator dispatches **B2 resume from W0.c re-execution**
on the post-B3 substrate. B2's W0.c brief is the same brief that
produced `21881591`; with the parser regression resolved, the brief
closes cleanly. B2 then continues W1 → W4 per its existing plan.

After B2 closes, **B4 opens** with a fresh plan for re-landing the W0'
work under the post-B2 xtask substrate. B4's plan is authored at that
point; this tranche does not pre-author B4.

The proc-macro contract returns to its idiomatic Rust scope at B2 close.
The runtime parser regression resolves at B4 close. AY-II.W1-W5 follows
from there. B3 is the necessary gate that lets that sequence execute on
honest substrates.
