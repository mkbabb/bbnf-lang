## Producer and carrier are different questions

An external receipt can be truthful for facts its producer controls or
directly observes. A build orchestrator can seal its own absolute Cargo
identity, command, canonical environment, selected input/tool ledgers, start,
completion, and Cargo status. Merely invoking Cargo and inspecting its final
exit/artifact does not additionally prove rustc's resolved linker child,
physical argv/response branch, or that child's status.

A causal observer at the rustc spawn boundary, or sufficiently authoritative
external process instrumentation, could produce those link-child facts. No
allowed stable Cargo/rustc callback was proved to expose the complete event.
That makes an external receipt a viable conceptual producer only under an
unselected observation mechanism, not an implementation-ready route.

A canonical namespace in an existing ledger could carry a digest/reference to
an already truthful external event. It cannot manufacture that event. Current
`ToolBinding` lacks attempt identity, producer/time, response bytes, status,
and artifact relation; the exact-six build-tool hash also hashes capability
bindings rather than link attempts. Reusing those meanings for BuildLinkLaunch
would be overload. The current embedded ledger remains a useful pre-build
carrier, not a complete event producer.

The author's proposed "minimum truthful receipt" is not itself proved
sufficient. It records the immediate rustc child while author lines 352-363
acknowledge that a compiler driver may construct another linker child that
rustc does not expose. Adjudication must define whether BuildLinkLaunch ends at
rustc's immediate child or includes every child that performs the link. Until
then the proposed minimum is research prose, not a selected schema or future
authority.
