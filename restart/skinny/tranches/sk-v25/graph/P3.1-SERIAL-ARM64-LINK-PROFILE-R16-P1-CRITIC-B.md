## Correct `/usr/bin/time -l` semantics

Apple `time` forks/execs Cargo, samples `proc_pid_rusage(pid, V4)` for that
immediate Cargo PID, then obtains a `struct rusage` from `wait4(pid, ...)`.
XNU rolls a process's already reaped child usage into its final rusage. Most
counters and CPU times are additive; `ruadd` specifically combines
`ru_maxrss` by **maximum**, not addition.

Consequently `2,981,249,024 maximum resident set size` is about 2.98 GB decimal
(2.78 GiB) and is a max-reduced high-water value across Cargo and usage rolled
up from terminated descendants. It is not the sum of simultaneous RSS, not a
timeline, and not a process identity. It cannot say whether Cargo, rustc, a
compiler/linker process, or another descendant supplied the maximum. This was
a successful P1 tree; the number cannot identify either historically killed
R15 process, a signal sender, or a cause.

The last three rows are a different surface. `instructions retired`, `cycles
elapsed`, and `peak memory footprint` come from `proc_pid_rusage` of the
immediate Cargo PID, not the `wait4` rusage rollup. The `79,217,312` peak
footprint therefore does not contradict the 2.98 GB tree maximum. The author's
blanket statement that the entire block is “accumulated for the terminated
Cargo descendant tree” is **INCORRECT**.

Likewise, 244 signals received is an accumulated delivery count, not evidence
of SIGKILL, its target, or its sender. Zero swaps and zero block-I/O rows do not
prove no memory pressure or no sccache-daemon/cache I/O.
